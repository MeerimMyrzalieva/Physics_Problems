# Problem 8: Circular Motion (Centripetal Acceleration at the Equator)

We consider a person standing on the Earth's equator.
Due to Earth's rotation, the person moves in a circle around the Earth's axis.

Given:

Earth radius:

$$
R = 6378,\text{km} = 6.378 \times 10^6,\text{m}
$$

Rotation period (one day):

$$
T = 24,\text{h} = 86400,\text{s}
$$

We want to find the centripetal acceleration.

---

## 1) Angular Velocity

Angular velocity is:

$$
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T}
$$

Substitute:

$$
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{86400}
\approx 7.27 \times 10^{-5},\text{s}^{-1}
$$

---

## 2) Centripetal Acceleration Formula

For circular motion:

$$
a = \omega^2 R
$$

---

## 3) Substitute Values

$$
a = (7.27 \times 10^{-5})^2 \cdot 6.378 \times 10^6
$$

Compute:

$$
a \approx 0.034,\text{m/s}^2
$$

---

## Final Answer

The centripetal acceleration at the Earth's equator is:

$$
\boxed{a \approx 0.034,\text{m/s}^2}
$$

This is much smaller than gravitational acceleration (g = 9.8,\text{m/s}^2).
